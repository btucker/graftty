Strip cards: two fixes

The clash was a strip that runs left to right with cards that stack top to bottom. Either the cards turn to match the strip, or the strip turns to match the cards.

1. Cards follow the strip — a row, left to right

The cards line up under the strip in the same order as the marks. The one under the pointer is largest; its neighbours shrink to each side. The row spills left over the terminal as far as it needs.

2. The strip turns upright — a small rail on each tile's edge

Each tile gets a short vertical rail on its left edge instead of a line underneath. Time runs downward everywhere, and the cards stack beside the tile exactly as they do beside the main rail.

The trade

1 keeps your line-under-the-tile and gives the strip the tile's full width, about 200 points, so marks are easier to tell apart. Its cost is a new card layout: text in a row of narrow cards wraps more.

2 makes every agent's history read the same way and reuses the rail's card stack unchanged. Its cost is resolution: a tile is only about 70 points tall, a third of the width, so a long history crowds sooner.
